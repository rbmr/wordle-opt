# Diagnose History

Generated by `cargo run --release -- diagnose [-n N1,N2,...]` (see
`src/main.rs::run_diagnose`). Reports the full `Metrics` breakdown (max
branching factor `k`, max recursion depth reached, states/guesses
evaluated, cache hits, and both prune counters) for one random sample per
size - the extra signal needed to explain *why* wall-clock time scales
the way it does, when `benchmark_history.md`'s cost/time numbers alone
aren't enough to tell.

Uses its own fixed seed (`DIAGNOSE_SEED`), independent of
`benchmark-random`'s `BENCHMARK_RANDOM_SEED` - the two never draw the same
sample sequence, so don't expect a size's numbers here to match a
same-sized row in `benchmark_history.md`.

## Diagnose Run: commit=f618964 host=ubuntu-main seed=20260910 sizes=[750, 1000, 1100, 1200, 1300, 1400]

> Note on provenance: file-logging (writing to `diagnose_history.md`) was
> added to `run_diagnose` in the commit right after this run was kicked
> off at `f618964`, so this specific run's numbers were captured from its
> terminal output rather than written automatically by the tool.
> Transcribed verbatim, not retyped from memory. Every run after this one
> writes here automatically.

Investigation into why `benchmark-random` showed avg cost/candidate
scaling smoothly from N=1000 to N=1500 while wall time jumped ~25x (see
issue #3). Answer: `max_depth` - the deepest guess-sequence the search
had to explore to prove optimality - steps from 4 to 5 between N=1100 and
N=1200, and that step, not smooth growth in N, is what dominates:

| Size | MaxK | Depth | Cost | Time(s) | States | Guesses | CacheHit | EquivPrn | BndsPrn |
|------|------|-------|------|---------|--------|---------|----------|----------|---------|
| 750 | 115 | 4 | 2282 | 71.926 | 135474 | 208063 | 228449 | 931524567 | 340471040 |
| 1000 | 124 | 4 | 3133 | 284.483 | 578872 | 890564 | 910717 | 4019518279 | 1414537770 |
| 1100 | 128 | 4 | 3477 | 369.320 | 763989 | 1153426 | 1211204 | 5359420314 | 1744193481 |
| 1200 | 128 | 5 | 3823 | 1918.391 | 2735481 | 3725692 | 2371173 | 15643910366 | 4068465206 |
| 1300 | 134 | 5 | 4187 | 2453.222 | 3821764 | 5821083 | 4782849 | 23100559259 | 7653214643 |
| 1400 | 134 | 5 | 4553 | 4663.315 | 6700067 | 10266442 | 8584829 | 39914037080 | 11550641243 |

Cache hit rate (`cache_hits / (cache_hits + states_evaluated)`) drops from
~61-63% at depth 4 (750-1100) to ~46-56% at depth 5 (1200-1400) - the
mechanism: transposition-table reuse depends on different guess orderings
converging on the same candidate subset, and the number of distinct
subsets reachable grows combinatorially with depth, so reuse gets rarer
each level deeper. Cost-per-candidate barely moves across the same
transition (2282/750=3.04 -> 4553/1400=3.25) because only a minority of
candidates in the sample actually need the 5th guess - but the search
still has to exhaustively verify a 4-guess solution isn't optimal for the
*whole* set before concluding a 5th guess is needed, which is what
balloons.

Implication for the N=2340/10-hour goal: this is a step function, not a
smooth curve. Each new depth threshold the full-scale search crosses
(and it will almost certainly need to reach 5, likely 6, for the hardest
real Wordle answers) will cost a similar disproportionate jump. Improving
cache/transposition-table reuse at depth, or reducing how often the
search needs to prove a shallower solution impossible before trying
deeper, is likely a higher-leverage target than per-node micro-optimization.
